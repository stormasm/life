
Last evening we went to the Cormac event at OSU and the author of his latest
biography gave a talk.

It turns out the evening turned into quite an affair for many reasons
and the author of the book, as noted in the NYT book review was himself
a character in many ways.

As noted in the book review


> On the downside, the book is hampered by consistent intrusions of flowery language, as if the biographer got too excited and tried to compete with his subject. 


And that is why the talk last night was so good.  Because Tracy really got into
his subject not only when he read the book but how he showed us his sketch book
of his outline and notes before he started the writing project.

At the end of the event I asked the second question:

"How many people who Cormac knew well were you able to speak with"

> It also imparts the slightly hollow sense that for every person willing to talk with Daugherty, 10 hung up the phone.


And his response was in line with the above quote from the book review.

Tracy expanded on another key point from my question.

He said that you have to be careful when you talk to people because their
perspective on who the person was and who the person actually is can be quite
different.  For example, even close family members who thought they knew a person
it turns out later did not know that person at all.

Kind of reminiscent of life in general how we do not even really know ourselves
very well and that our actions and ways of doing things are decoupled from
how we perceive ourselves.

Through out the evening Tracy continued to emphasize a lot of points about
being a writer and what it takes to write well.

Although the talk was about Cormac, Tracy took a lot of time at the beginning
of the talk to talk about writing in general.

He said that writing is a very physical activity even though people think
of it as a pure mental activity.  He said the best writers probably see
the physical aspect of the writing more importantly.  He showed us his
sketch book and how the movement of the hands etc. was important.

For me, its more about how creative people in general get their best
ideas and thoughts when they are not actually sitting there writing
but more so going for long hikes in the woods or in your meditation as
you walk around your neighborhood etc.

That happened to me yesterday.  I was working on a problem and Hb and
I went out for a walk and I came home and wrote down the ideas that I 
discovered `along the way` and that was what got printed so to speak.

### Gary Fisketjon

At the end of the talk by Tracy and before handing it over to the audience
for questions he let the audience know that a special guest was here and
none other than the editor of Cormacs books.

Gary too was quite the character.

He told us how one time while he was editing a book by Cormac that he told Cormac
that he was inconsistent in how he wrote `1874` because sometime he would spell
out eighteen seventy four and sometimes he would not and Gary said to Cormac what
should we do to be consistent ?

And Cormac said write all of the dates out by hand :)

### Mike Parker

This year I have really got into Beavers Football and along with that listening
to the games on the internet radio.

- [Mike Parker - Voice of the Beavers](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=mike+parker+the+voice+of+the+beavers)

So last night the person who asked a question after me was this voice
behind me who I knew was very familiar.  When I turned around to look and
see who was asking the question it was none other than Mike Parker.

I had never met him before or even seen him in person so after the event
concluded I went up to him and said hi and we luckily got into a long talk
about a pet peeve of mine that I have been chasing down with the Oregon State
Athletic Dept for months now.

Turns out the microphones on the press conferences that JaMarcus Shepherd does
on Mondays are not working properly.  So you can hear Shep talking but you can
not always hear what questions are being asked.

Mike Parker told me he will check it out on Monday but he knew what I was talking
about because he believes the mics are not calibrated properly and he will attempt
to bring this to the attention of the audio folks who do the interviews.

### PS

It turns out that both of the `famous` people in the room were from Oregon.

Tracy lives a few blocks down the street from OSU campus and Gary lives in Salem.

So that made the event last evening more special.  Knowing that I could possibly
bump into Tracy sometime walking around town or campus.

After the talk was over and everyone had cleared out there were only a few
people remaining.  Everyone's books had been signed and they were turning off
the lights so to speak I got to speak briefly with Tracy.

I told him I did not think the Vanity Fair article would get in the way of
Cormac's legacy and he basically agreed.  That was also noted in other places
saying that great artists through out the course of human history are noted for
their work --- not for the personal preferences they choose along the way in their life.
