Year-of-Death RMD Rules

* **The Requirement:** The beneficiary still must satisfy the deceased person's unfulfilled RMD for the year of death. \[[1](https://www.stwserve.com/new-irs-regulations-on-year-of-death-rmds/), [2](https://thelink.ascensus.com/articles/2023/8/11/rmd-rules-when-an-ira-owner-dies)\]  
* **The Deadline Change:** The deadline to take this final distribution is now the later of December 31 of the year following the year of death, or the beneficiary's tax filing deadline (including extensions) for the year of death. \[[1](https://ritaus.org/understanding-the-new-year-of-death-rmd-rules/)\]  
* **Penalty Protection:** Missing a year-of-death RMD triggers an automatic waiver of the standard 25% excise tax penalty if corrected by the extended deadline.  
   \[[1](https://phillipslytle.com/new-irs-regulations-on-year-of-death-rmds/)\]

**Yes, you must still fulfill the year-of-death Required Minimum Distribution (RMD)** if the original owner died after reaching their mandatory RMD age (currently 73\) and had not yet taken it for this year. \[[1](https://www.aarp.org/money/personal-finance/inherited-ira-distribution-advice/), [2](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1503ee86-81ed-4498-ae43-b3cce54960c4)\]

However, under the **IRS final regulations**, the specific rule regarding **how long you have to pay it** and the penalties for missing it have significantly changed in your favor

The Finalized Year-of-Death RMD Rules

Historically, if an account owner died without taking their RMD, the beneficiary faced a strict deadline of December 31 of that same year to take the distribution, or face a steep penalty. The current rules provide a major relief window: \[[1](https://ritaus.org/understanding-the-new-year-of-death-rmd-rules/), [2](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1503ee86-81ed-4498-ae43-b3cce54960c4)\]

* **Extended Deadline:** You no longer have to rush to take the deceased owner's unfulfilled RMD by December 31 of this year. The deadline is now the **later of**:  
  1. **December 31 of the year *following* the year of death** (which will be December 31, 2027).  
  2. Your own personal tax filing deadline (including extensions) for the year of death. \[[1](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1503ee86-81ed-4498-ae43-b3cce54960c4), [2](https://ritaus.org/understanding-the-new-year-of-death-rmd-rules/)\]  
*   
* **Automatic Penalty Waiver:** If you fail to withdraw the decedent's final RMD before the end of this calendar year, the IRS grants an **automatic waiver** of the standard 25% excise tax penalty, provided you empty that specific year-of-death RMD amount by the extended deadline noted above. \[[1](https://phillipslytle.com/new-irs-regulations-on-year-of-death-rmds/), [2](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=1503ee86-81ed-4498-ae43-b3cce54960c4)\]  
* **Flexible Distribution (Multiple Beneficiaries):** If there are multiple beneficiaries on the account, the unfulfilled year-of-death RMD does not need to be split proportionally. The IRS allows *any* combination of the beneficiaries to take the distribution, as long as the total required amount is taken from the account
