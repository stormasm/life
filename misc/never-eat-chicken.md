Yes, Costco's rotisserie chicken contains a few specific elements—most notably **high levels of sodium** and **sodium phosphate additives**—that can contribute to an increase in blood pressure.

### **Key Factors That Can Affect Blood Pressure:**

> 1. **High Sodium Content (The Primary Factor)**  
   * **Injected Brine Solution:** To keep the meat juicy and tender under warming lamps, Costco injects its rotisserie chickens with a saline solution.  
   * **The Numbers:** A standard 3-ounce serving of Costco rotisserie chicken contains roughly **460 mg of sodium**. That represents about **20% to 30% of the recommended daily limit** (the American Heart Association recommends limiting sodium to 1,500–2,300 mg per day, especially for individuals with hypertension).  
   * **Why Sodium Raises Blood Pressure:** Excess sodium causes your body to hold onto extra water to wash the salt out of your system. This extra fluid increases blood volume, forcing your heart to work harder and putting greater pressure on your blood vessels.  
> 2. **Sodium Phosphate Additives**  
   * The injection solution also includes **sodium phosphates** (used as a moisture binder and texture enhancer). Beyond adding to the overall sodium load, dietary inorganic phosphates can affect vascular health and fluid regulation in some individuals.  
> 3. **Serving Size Portions**  
   * A typical 3-ounce serving is relatively small (about the size of a deck of cards or a small palm). It is very easy to eat two or three times that amount in a single sitting, which can quickly push your sodium intake over 1,000–1,500 mg in just one meal.

### **Tips to Reduce Blood Pressure Impact if You Eat It**

* **Remove the Skin:** While much of the brine penetrates the meat, a significant portion of the salt and spices is concentrated on the skin. Removing the skin can reduce some sodium intake.  
* **Balance Your Meal with Potassium:** Potassium helps your kidneys flush out extra sodium and relaxes blood vessel walls. Pair the chicken with potassium-rich foods like leafy greens, broccoli, sweet potatoes, or avocados.  
* **Watch Portion Sizes:** Stick to a 3- to 4-ounce serving rather than half or a quarter of the bird.  
* **Hydrate:** Drink plenty of water to help your body process the sodium.

If you have underlying hypertension, heart disease, or kidney concerns, raw chicken cooked at home with salt-free seasonings is a much safer low-sodium option than pre-packaged store-bought rotisserie birds.
