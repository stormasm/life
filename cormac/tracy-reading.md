
Last evening we went to the Cormac event at OSU and the author of his latest
biography gave a talk.

- [Cormac McCarthy: A Legacy Revisited, by Tracy Daugherty](https://tracydaugherty.com/nonfiction/cormac-mccarthy-a-legacy-revisited/)
- [Internet archive of the NYT Book Review](https://archive.ph/77Goz)

It turns out the evening turned into quite an affair for many reasons
and the author of the book, as noted in the NYT book review was himself
a character in many ways.

As noted in the book review

```rust
On the downside, it’s hampered by consistent intrusions of flowery language, as if the biographer got too excited and tried to compete with his subject. 
```

And that is why the talk last night was so good.  Because Tracy really got into
his subject not only when he read the book but how he showed us his sketch book
of his outline and notes before he started the writing project.

At the end of the event I asked the second question:

"How many people who Cormac knew well were you able to speak with"

```rust
It also imparts the slightly hollow sense that for every person willing to talk with Daugherty, 10 hung up the phone.
```

And his response was in line with the above quote from the book review.

Tracy expanded on another key point from my question.

He said that you have to be careful when you talk to people because their
perspective on who the person was and who the person actually is can be quite
different.  For example, even close family members who thought they knew a person
it turns out later did not know that person at all.

Kind of reminiscent of life in general how we do not even really know ourselves
very well and that our actions and ways of doing things are decoupled from
how we perceive ourselves.

### Mike Parker

This year I have really got into Beavers Football and along with that listening
to the games on the internet radio.

- [Mike Parker - Voice of the Beavers](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=mike+parker+the+voice+of+the+beavers)

So last night the person who asked a question after me was this voice
behind me who I knew was very familiar.  When I turned around to look and
see who was asking the question it was none other than Mike Parker.

I had never met him before or even seen him in person so after the event
concluded I went up to him and said hi and we luckily got into a long talk
about a pet peeve of mine that I have been chasing down with the Oregon State
Athletic Dept for months now.

Turns out the microphones on the press conferences that JaMarcus Shepherd does
on Mondays are not working properly.  So you can hear Shep talking but you can
not always hear what questions are being asked.

Mike Parker told me he will check it out on Monday but he knew what I was talking
about because he believes the mics are not calibrated properly and he will attempt
to bring this to the attention of the audio folks who do the interviews.
